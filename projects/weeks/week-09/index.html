THE MODEL, WITHOUT THE BLACK BOX

Count. Smooth. Compare.

Company speakers, two speaking contexts. Trained from the DF1 / DF2 earnings-call files supplied with the course notebook. A shared training vocabulary, Laplace smoothing, and equal class priors. No LLM is used for prediction.

score(c) = ln P(c) + ∑ n(w) · ln P(w | c)P(w | c) = (count(w, c) + 1) / (Nc + |V|)
A REPRODUCIBLE RECIPE

Inside the pipeline

  1. Label the source.Repair section boundaries, then identify company speakers from prepared turns and documented roster additions. Exclude analysts, operators, question readers, closing remarks and ambiguous mixed turns. Only management responses enter Q&A; short turns under 25 tokens are excluded from both classes.
  2. Clean the words.Lowercase English words; normalize curly apostrophes; retain contractions. Remove speaker labels and bracketed stage directions; remove identified legal-disclaimer sentences from Prepared. Numbers and punctuation are excluded. Keep stop words because they can carry speaking-style signals.
  3. Train two word distributions.Count all training words. Use one shared vocabulary and Laplace smoothing (α = 1). P(Prepared) = P(Q&A) = 0.5.
  4. Score a new sentence.Sum natural-log probabilities. The higher (less negative) score wins. Normalize scores with softmax to show model probabilities. Words outside the training vocabulary are ignored; no known words means no prediction.
HELD-OUT CHECK

Entire calls held out.

Split by company with seed 42: about 80% for training and 20% for testing. No company or call appears in both. Test sentences under 4 tokens and exact matches to training sentences are excluded. A deterministic punctuation rule protects common abbreviations.

A classroom experiment on the supplied transcripts. Topic, company and transcript style can affect predictions. Cleaning uses auditable rules and targeted manual fixes; upstream speaker labels may still contain errors. Sentences within a call are correlated. Probabilities are not calibrated confidence.
